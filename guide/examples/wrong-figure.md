# Illustrative example: wrong figure

**All scenario details below are synthetic and illustrative.**

## Scenario

A reviewer asks for a completion rate from a fictional training table: 24 of 30 participants completed the course.

## Flawed output

> “The completion rate was 80% of all 40 invited employees.”

The calculation 24 ÷ 30 equals 80%, but the denominator is enrolled participants, not 40 invitees.

## How the reviewer catches it

The reviewer checks the table definition and recomputes the figure. The table lists 30 enrolled participants; 40 is the number invited. The output silently changes the population.

## Corrected approach

Write: “24 of 30 enrolled participants completed the course (80%). The table does not state how many of the 40 invitees enrolled.” Keep the numerator, denominator, and population explicit.
