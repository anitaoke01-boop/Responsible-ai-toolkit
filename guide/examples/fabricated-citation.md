# Illustrative example: fabricated citation

**All scenario details below are synthetic and illustrative.**

## Scenario

A reviewer asks an AI assistant to draft a short note about a made-up workplace training pilot and requests citations. The assistant supplies a plausible-sounding research paper.

## Flawed output

> “A 2022 study by the fictional Northbridge Institute found that reminder emails improve course completion by 38% (Lee and Park, *Journal of Workplace Learning Metrics*, 14(2), 41–49).”

The named institute, journal, authors, and study are invented for this example. The output presents them as real evidence.

## How the reviewer catches it

The reviewer searches the exact title and citation, checks the publisher and library catalog, and finds no matching record. The supplied pilot notes also contain no such study or 38% result.

## Corrected approach

Delete the unsupported citation and number. Write: “The fictional pilot notes do not report a measured effect. We would need verified completion data and a comparison method before estimating an effect.” If evidence is needed, locate a real authoritative source and confirm it supports the specific claim.
